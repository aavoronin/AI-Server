Hardware Lead - Device Architect

Position summary
You design the board and overall device architecture for children's Artificial Intelligence (AI) hardware (Neybo) so components from different suppliers work as one system. You own architecture decisions and hardware sign-off; layout may be done with a contractor under your control.
Your scope runs from engineering decisions and schematic design through bring-up, factory iterations, and readiness for the first production batch. The production gate: no architecture rework after Design Validation Test (DVT); known issues accepted before production release.
You own the hardware end-to-end and are accountable for what goes into production.
Design device architecture and the interaction logic between components from multiple suppliers.
Develop schematics and Printed Circuit Board (PCB) layout (or tightly control a layout contractor), run bring-up and debugging on prototypes.
Close PCB iterations with the factory: Design for Manufacturability (DFM) reviews, revisions, production documentation alignment.
Prepare hardware for the first batch: tests, acceptance criteria, quality control at production start.
Document full device architecture and logic so the team and the factory share one source of truth.
Required qualifications
7+ years in electronic device development; at least 2 completed projects where you were architect or lead from schematic through first production batch.
End-to-end device architecture: block diagram, schematic, Microcontroller Unit / System on Chip (MCU/SoC) selection, power, mixed-signal, and interfaces; ability to build a Bill of Materials (BOM) from multiple suppliers with approved alternates.
Hands-on schematic and Printed Circuit Board (PCB) in Altium Designer (or Allegro/Cadence at a comparable level): schematic capture, layout or tight control of a layout contractor; production-ready output (Gerber files, Bill of Materials (BOM), pick-and-place, assembly drawings).
Bench bring-up and testing: electrical, functional, and integration tests; basic thermal checks on critical nodes; results documented before handoff to manufacturing.
Contract Manufacturer (CM) New Product Introduction (NPI) experience: closed PCB iterations with a contract manufacturer (Design for Manufacturability (DFM) review, revisions, first-batch control) - not just "sent files and waited for boards."
Engineering Validation Test / Design Validation Test (EVT/DVT) stages (or equivalent): prototype -> validated board -> readiness for production batch without architecture rework.
Electromagnetic Compatibility / Electrostatic Discharge (EMC/ESD) at architecture level: routing, filtering, and testability designed in before serial build, not fixed on the final board.
Full Contract Manufacturer (CM) production documentation package: component specification, test procedures, production release.
Portfolio with artifacts: photos/descriptions of assembled boards, sample schematics and PCB layouts, a first-batch case study - your role, timeline, and what you personally delivered.
English B2+ (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages): datasheets, supplier and factory communication, technical specifications.
Nice to have
Consumer devices, wearables, or smart-device experience.
Coordination with embedded firmware on the Hardware / Firmware (HW/FW) boundary.
Factory ramp experience with manufacturers in Asia.
Preparation for certification before serial launch (Federal Communications Commission (FCC), Conformité Européenne (CE), or equivalent).
Success metrics
Approved architecture + Bill of Materials (BOM): tested component and interface list from multiple suppliers.
Schematics + tested, approved Printed Circuit Board (PCB) ready for the industrial batch.
Closed PCB iterations with the contract manufacturer (Design for Manufacturability (DFM) + revisions).
Electrical, thermal, functional, and integration test results documented.
Contract Manufacturer (CM) production documentation package signed off.
Final hardware acceptance for the first production batch.
