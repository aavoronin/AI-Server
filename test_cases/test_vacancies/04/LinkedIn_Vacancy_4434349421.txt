AKA The Agile Recruiter 🤝🏾🚀 Getting you out of Recruitment Paralysis, and honing your processes to get your teams delivering better value quicker, and taking the headache out of Agile, Delivery & Product Recruitment.
1d •
If you saw my post yesterday, or followed me for a while, you've probably heard me talk about 𝗥𝗲𝗰𝗿𝘂𝗶𝘁𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗣𝗮𝗿𝗮𝗹𝘆𝘀𝗶𝘀.But I wanted to explain what it is, why it's getting worse and what we can do about it.For me, Recruitment Paralysis is what happens when both sides of the hiring market become overwhelmed and increasingly ineffective at connecting with each other.𝗖𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗶𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝘀𝗶𝗱𝗲:More competition → More applications → Less response → Apply to more roles → Lower quality applications → More competition.𝗛𝗶𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘀𝗶𝗱𝗲:More applications → More CVs → Less time per application → More filtering → Good candidates get missed → Roles stay open → More applications.Round and round we go 🔄😵‍💫Candidates feel like there are no jobs.Businesses feel like there are no good candidates.Recruiters/TA are stuck in the middle, trying to make sense of the noise.𝗦𝗼 𝘄𝗵𝘆 𝗶𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝗴𝗲𝘁𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝘀𝗲?• AI makes it incredibly easy to apply for hundreds of jobs• AI-generated applications make differentiation harder• One-click applications have removed almost all friction• Vacancies receive hundreds of applications within hours• Recruiters/TA don't have capacity to properly assess everyone• Businesses are becoming increasingly specific about what "good" looks like• An abundance of options creates decision paralysis• More technology is being introduced to manage the volumeWe're increasingly using technology to deal with problems that were in part created by technology.And somewhere amongst all of that...There's a human trying to find a job and a hiring manager trying to find a human.So what can we do?𝗛𝗶𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴?Be realistic about non-negotiables, give proper feedback, proactively find talent, move quickly and retain human judgement.𝗝𝗼𝗯 𝗵𝘂𝗻𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴?Be targeted, build relationships with recruiters in your market, use your network and make it easy for people to understand what you do.𝗥𝗲𝗰𝗿𝘂𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴?Know your market, know your candidates, challenge unrealistic briefs, give honest feedback, build communities and actually connect people.Our job can't become filtering applications and forwarding CVs; otherwise, AI can do it.Unless candidates, recruiters, TA and hiring managers change how we operate, Recruitment Paralysis will only get worse.And the faster AI ramps up, the quicker this problem will compound.So...𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗱𝗼 𝗬𝗢𝗨 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸 𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗱𝘀 𝘁𝗼 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗲?Candidates, Recruiters, TA and Hiring Wanted to share a real-life example of how internal processes can completely derail a hire...A business spots a genuine need in the team and engages us on it.• We go to market✅• Great candidate identified ✅• Interviews done ✅• Offer made and accepted ✅All within a week...Job done...right?Nope! 🙃What followed was almost 2 months of back and forth just trying to get recruitment terms agreed and signed off 🫠Mind you, terms were already in place; they were just expired and needed a new signature and a few minor wording/clause changes.Meanwhile, there’s a candidate who’s accepted the job and is just waiting to start their next chapter.Fast forward a bit...Terms are finally sorted.And the candidate rightly decided they couldn’t wait any longer and went back to market 🤷🏾‍♂️In the time it took to get the terms sorted, the candidate went through an entire permanent recruitment process elsewhere, interviewed, was offered, accepted AND agreed a start date...All before the onboarding for this role had even got underway 🤯So...We go back to market and restart the search.• More candidate calls• More CV reviews• More interviewsAnd now the role has been put on hold due to changes in the team🤦🏾‍♂️Now this isn't me just complaining; these things happen... But there's lots of lessons to take from this.The biggest one for me is pretty simple...Being able to move quickly in hiring and procurement is just as important as being agile within your teams and wider org.Just think about the cost of what happened here:• Time spent sourcing and interviewing candidates• Hiring manager time• Recruitment/TA time• Procurement and legal time• A great candidate lost• The whole search starting again• And the original business problem still not solvedThere absolutely needs to be governance around hiring, procurement, and suppliers.But those processes should be in place to enable the business to move quickly, not slow it down.Because good talent isn’t going to sit around for two months waiting for internal processes to catch up.And the irony is, businesses will often spend months trying to hire people to make them more agile...While their hiring processes are anything but.There’s definitely a balance to strike.Governance & Agility are both a necessity in a growing business, but you shouldn't sacrifice one in favour of the other.Have you ever missed out on a role or key hire due to long-drawn internal processes? Scrolling LinkedIn the other day, I came across two posts back-to-back from some really respected recruiters in the industry.Both were sharing examples of poor candidate experience.One candidate was repeatedly called by the wrong name throughout the interview process.The other reached a final interview only to be met with a lack of attention and a dismissive attitude.Reading them back-to-back, I couldn’t help but think…We’re slipping backwards when it comes to candidate experience.I’ve said it before, and I’ll say it again…When you’re hiring:• Your candidates are your customers.• Your hiring process is your product.Candidate experience isn’t just about being polite.It’s a reflection of your organisation.I’m sure your business wouldn’t repeatedly call a customer by the wrong name or dismiss them during an important meeting.Whether someone joins your business or not, they’ll leave with an impression of your brand.In a market where reviews, referrals and personal networks carry so much weight, those experiences travel.A good candidate experience can lead to:• Candidates reapplying in the future.• Referrals from people who weren’t even hired.• Stronger employer branding.• Higher offer acceptance rates.• Better relationships with recruitment partners.And a poor one?The opposite of everything above…In addition to candidates and recruiters being far more likely to share the bad experience than a good one.People don’t forget how they were made to feel.Most candidates don’t expect every interview to end with an offer.But they do expect to be treated with respect, listened to and kept informed.Those things cost nothing.Whether you’re a hiring manager, internal TA or an agency recruiter, we all play a huge part in creating a positive experience.So I’m keen to hear some recent examples…What’s the best (or worst) candidate experience you’ve had?Be interesting to see what’s winning out there right now💡#Recruitment#Hiring#CandidateExperience#EmployerBrand#TheAgileRecruiter…more

Feedback. It's the lifeblood of recruitment.Whether you're a candidate, a recruiter, an internal TA team or a hiring manager, how are you supposed to improve without it?I genuinely don't understand why it's become so difficult to give.If someone has taken the time to prepare for an interview, turn up, answer your questions and invest their time in your business, they deserve feedback.Likewise, if you're rejecting CVs from an agency or your internal TA team, tell them why.Otherwise they'll keep searching based on the information they have.They'll keep sending profiles they think fit the brief.You'll keep rejecting them.Everyone gets frustrated.Everyone wastes time.The best recruitment processes I've been involved in have one thing in common...Honest, timely feedback.Sometimes it's as simple as:• "Too delivery focused."• "Needs stronger stakeholder management."• "Salary is too high."• "Not enough financial services experience."It's not rocket science. And those few simple words can completely change the next profiles that come through.The same goes for candidates.Some of the best candidates I've worked with are the ones who've taken feedback on board, worked on it, and come back stronger next time around.Nobody gets better in a vacuum.Feedback is how we improve.It helps candidates interview better.It helps recruiters refine their search.It helps hiring managers sense-check what the market is telling them.And ultimately, it helps businesses make better hires.So next time you're rejecting a CV or finishing an interview, spend the extra couple of minutes giving some meaningful feedback.It might save you and everyone else involved hours of time further down the line.How important do you think feedback is in recruitment? Have you seen it getting better or worse over the past few years?#Recruitment#Hiring#CandidateExperience#Feedback#TheAgileRecruiter…more
